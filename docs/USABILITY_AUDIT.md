# Usability and study-flow audit

November 13, 2026 · Android 0.2.0 and desktop 0.4.2

The first fixes should protect the wording and references a reader takes away. The interlinear can put the wrong English phrase under a Greek verb. The desktop importer can silently remove letters. Notes can disappear from a chapter’s study panel or be attached to the wrong passage. On the phone, the biggest everyday improvement would be returning readers to the exact search, word map, or tool they just left.

## Scope and evidence

The Android walkthrough used the installed app on a Samsung SM-S936U running Android 17, with a 384 × 832 CSS-pixel viewport. The desktop walkthrough used a fresh build from the 0.4.2 working tree with its bundled corpus and disposable study profiles. The copy in this Mac’s Applications folder reports 0.4.1. The phone was returned to its starting passage and reading mode.

**Walkthrough** means the behavior was exercised through the interface. **Isolated reproduction** means the actual query, importer, or renderer component was exercised with a known passage or a disposable fixture. **Code review** identifies a path without claiming someone completed it on hardware. Earlier Android upgrade and backup checks are identified separately. These were evaluator walkthroughs. Task-completion rates, one-handed use by participants, and TalkBack or VoiceOver speech output still need direct user testing.

## Fix first

| Priority and scope | What the reader experiences | Acceptance check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Wrong interlinear pairing · both** | In John 17:8, choose the critical Greek text and stack BSB. The translation row puts “I have given” below the form meaning “You gave,” and vice versa. Both verbs are G1325. The code consumes translations in order of their Strong’s number, which fails when the English reorders two occurrences. Both installed Android and desktop APIs reproduced the reversal. The same mechanism swaps “tempter” and “tempted” in 1 Thessalonians 3:5. | In John 17:8, align ἔδωκάς to “You gave” and δέδωκα to “I have given” only where the edition supports those occurrence links. The BSB source rows are 97903 and 97905. Where identity is uncertain, display the complete labelled verse. Exercise both examples in the rendered stack and keep the restoration and copy regressions. |
| **2. Characters disappear during module import · desktop** | A real SQLite module containing `I am the &#x391; and the &#937;.` imported successfully as “I am the and the .” The original module still contains Α and Ω. The desktop importer substitutes a space for entities outside a short list. | The reader, search result and copied verse must preserve “I am the Α and the Ω.” Exercise the same module on both platforms, including decimal and hexadecimal encodings, ordinary ampersands and accents. Surface an error when the source cannot be interpreted faithfully. |
| **3. Unverified imported references are treated as equivalent · desktop, plus parallel scrolling on both** | With a fixture using BSB Philippians 1:16 numbering, desktop offered a shared note at KJV 1:16. The known BSB counterpart is KJV 1:17. The note write and corresponding same-number cross-reference lookup were reproduced in isolation. Bookmarking follows the same mapping. Android explicitly withholds these actions for imports, but both readers still use the same-number fallback when synchronizing columns. | An import with unknown numbering can be read and highlighted in its own edition. A shared note, bookmark, or synchronized scroll uses an established correspondence or explains why one is unavailable. A verified bundled BSB column still aligns to KJV 1:17. |
| **4. Saved notes are missing from their native chapter · both** | A note on WEB Romans 14:24 is stored at KJV 16:25. Reopening WEB Romans 14 fetches notes for KJV Romans 14 and omits the note from the chapter list and verse marker. GNV Daniel 3:31 → KJV 4:1 reproduces it too. Android’s individual verse sheet can recover the note. The desktop popover can offer to create another. Markdown export also puts the WEB highlight and the canonically stored note under different chapter headings without explaining the numbering of the note. | Save, reopen, edit and export one note in each example. Show one note in the correct native chapter, keep its highlight tied to the edition, and label the reference system wherever the export uses canonical numbers. |
| **5. Editing actions can discard writing · desktop** | In the live app, typing a verse note and choosing yellow in the same popover saved the highlight, dismissed the editor, and left zero notes. A separate notebook component test showed that applying an AI rewrite after typing another sentence replaced the newer sentence. | After applying a highlight, the note draft remains available. An AI proposal remembers the revision it was based on. Applying it after a subsequent edit offers comparison or explicit replacement. Test both with delayed saves. |

The interlinear example is a study-display defect. Verified restoration has a separate occurrence-specific path. In BSB 1 John 2:1, the existing Android checks distinguish ἁμάρτητε and ἁμάρτῃ and preserve “not.” Preserve that behavior while fixing the stacked English row. Walkthroughs covered the reader and parallels, interlinear, restored-word comparisons, concordance, frequency maps, search, passage tools, place and person directories, Timeline, all four learning modules, annotations, notebook, and settings. The reading preference applies to scripture previews, including Hebrew and Greek. Fixed-size controls use a shared scale. A compact edition selector keeps space for the passage name when several translations are open.

At 320, 384 and 430 CSS pixels, the checked layouts stayed within the viewport. On an Android 36 emulator at system font scales of 100%, 130% and 200%, a Hebrew study form measured 30, 39 and 60 px, and a 15 px control measured 15, 19.5 and 30 px. Enlarged text exposed clipping in Preferred text order. Its edition names now wrap alongside the move controls. Android’s document and share pickers use the system’s typography.

The combined build passed 332 automated tests, 27 packaged macOS smoke checks, and all three connected Samsung checks. Two native instrumentation tests passed on the Android emulator. The corrected Android test build was installed over the prior version, and the Samsung was returned to its original reading preferences.

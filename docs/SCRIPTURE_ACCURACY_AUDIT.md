# Scripture accuracy audit

**2026-11-10 · Open Bible Study 0.3.1, local development build**

The audit compared the bundled SQLite corpus with the source records used to build it and with independently published Greek edition transcriptions. It also followed parallel passages, word replacements, cross-references and assistant requests through the application. All 167 automated tests passed on the build under review. This record describes behavior before the repairs prompted by the audit.

## Text and manuscript evidence

| Finding | Reproduction and reader impact |
| --- | --- |
| Swete’s Septuagint loses passages during import | The importer accepts only numeric chapter and verse fields. Across the 37 input files, it skips **5,124 nonempty source lines at 237 distinct labels in 10 books**. These include the Esther prologue and 80 lines at Job 2:9a–d. The displayed Job 2:9 ends midway through the wife’s speech. The count measures source lines, including editorial markers. A parser must preserve native subdivisions, even when they lack an English equivalent. [Job source, beginning of the omitted passage](https://github.com/nathans/lxx-swete/blob/26bad3eb42bba98471d154c954e36a6f30a0279d/data/32.Job.txt#L782) · [Swete importer](../data-pipeline/build.ts#L859) |
| Editorial matter appears as Scripture | Five entire LXX verse records contain a Roman chapter numeral alone. Exodus 20:1 reads **XX**. Numbers 17:1 and 19:1 and 1 Kings 14:1 and 16:1 have the same failure. At 2 Kings 23:31, editorial sigla and the Latin apparatus abbreviations *om* and *pr* appear among the Greek words. The digitized input already contains these artifacts. Filtering numerals alone would leave the mixed passages corrupted. Compare the readings with a verified witness and distinguish editorial content before indexing. [2 Kings source](https://github.com/nathans/lxx-swete/blob/26bad3eb42bba98471d154c954e36a6f30a0279d/data/14.Regnorum_IV.txt#L16982) · [Reading-edition assembly](../data-pipeline/build.ts#L985) |
| The edition labeled WLC incorporates reconstructions | The TAHOT source marks **152 words in 36 verses** as Hebrew reconstructions from the Greek (type X) and **27 words in three verses** as restorations from parallel Hebrew passages (type R). The importer stores these in the main text and discards the distinction. At Genesis 4:8 it displays Cain saying “let us go into the field.” At 1 Samuel 13:1 it displays the reconstructed numbers thirty and forty. A reader needs to know which witness supplies these words. Preserve the source types and distinguish the translator’s composite from a WLC transcription in reading, search and quotations. [TAHOT methodology](https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data/blob/f71b5e51f2d768ae72e3ed69743a7a9df25ef8b5/Translators%20Amalgamated%20OT%2BNT/TAHOT%20Gen-Deu%20-%20Translators%20Amalgamated%20Hebrew%20OT%20-%20STEPBible.org%20CC%20BY.txt#L23) · [Hebrew importer](../data-pipeline/build.ts#L797) |
| Reconstructed Greek word order differs from the named editions | A verse-by-verse comparison with the publisher’s plain-text Scrivener 1894 and Robinson–Pierpont 2005 editions found **104 TR verse pairs and 66 Byzantine verse pairs** with precisely the same words in a different order. These edition-specific counts can overlap. Both reconstructed editions place two occurrences of ἐσμέν together at the end of 1 Corinthians 15:19. Both publisher texts place the first immediately after ἠλπικότες. John 7:31 provides a shorter check. The app begins δὲ πολλοί while both reference editions begin πολλοὶ δέ. Validate reorderings against the edition being displayed. [Publisher’s verse files](https://www.byztxt.com/downloads.html) · [Order calculation](../data-pipeline/step.ts#L64) |
| The Greek apparatus suppresses differences that change the meaning | An exhaustive NA/TR comparison found **869 verses** where the apparatus sees all words as shared even though the Greek letters differ. In **430** of these verses, the differing forms also have different morphology tags. At 1 Corinthians 1:15, the app’s Greek text and TAGNT both distinguish “you were baptized” from “I baptized.” Both forms carry G907, so the comparison drops the verse. At 1 John 2:20 it loses “all of you” versus “all things.” Compare the source forms and retain morphology when aligning related words. [TAGNT source](https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data/blob/f71b5e51f2d768ae72e3ed69743a7a9df25ef8b5/Translators%20Amalgamated%20OT%2BNT/TAGNT%20Act-Rev%20-%20Translators%20Amalgamated%20Greek%20NT%20-%20STEPBible.org%20CC-BY.txt#L44159) · [Apparatus comparison](../src/main/db/bible.ts#L694) |
| Both sides of a Hebrew variant open the same dictionary entry | In Genesis 27:3 the Qere (reading) is “game,” H6718. The Ketiv (written form) is “food,” H6720. Both links open H6718. Across stored variants, 256 records had a single identifiable Ketiv root tag in the source that differed from the stored shared tag. Preserve each reading’s lexical identifier. [Genesis 27:3 source](https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data/blob/f71b5e51f2d768ae72e3ed69743a7a9df25ef8b5/Translators%20Amalgamated%20OT%2BNT/TAHOT%20Gen-Deu%20-%20Translators%20Amalgamated%20Hebrew%20OT%20-%20STEPBible.org%20CC%20BY.txt#L17118) · [Apparatus rows](../src/main/db/bible.ts#L743) |

## Word-level accuracy

**Septuagint glosses inherit unrelated New Testament contexts.** At Genesis 1:1 the interlinear gloss for ἐποίησεν is “committed,” from a different use of the verb. The app assigns that exact gloss to 608 LXX tokens. It assigns inferred tags to 388,659 LXX tokens overall. Neither count is an error rate. The tagging procedure chooses the most frequent Strong’s number for a normalized NT form, then uses the first NT gloss and morphology it encountered for that choice. Label inference wherever those tags appear. A passage-specific gloss requires evidence for the word in that passage. [Tag selection](../data-pipeline/build.ts#L1116) · [Reader’s gloss](../src/main/db/bible.ts#L588)

**The word “one” opens the entry for “into” in Greek Deuteronomy 6:4.** The LXX tagger folds accents and breathings before choosing a lexical number. All 297 exact occurrences of εἷς in the bundled LXX received G1519, the preposition. All 680 occurrences of ἢ (“or”) received G3588, the article. The concordance counts these assignments as occurrences. Preserve significant spelling distinctions and identify ambiguous analyses explicitly before using them in a word study. [LXX tagging](../data-pipeline/build.ts) · [Concordance](../src/main/db/bible.ts)

**The Hebrew concordance omits tagged word components, and the lexicon can hide existing entries.** The TAHOT row for the first word of Genesis tags both the prefix (“in”, H9003) and the root (“beginning”, H7225). The importer keeps a single number. The Hebrew concordance returns zero results for H9003 despite the prefix appearing on 15,777 source words. At Genesis 1:11 the packaged app returned a scholarly entry for H9033 and no base Strong’s entry. The lexicon pane gated the scholarly result on the base entry. Thirteen Hebrew and 80 Greek vocabulary cards consequently had a numeric identifier for a front and a missing answer despite a scholarly entry being present. [Hebrew importer and vocabulary builder](../data-pipeline/build.ts) · [Lexicon pane](../src/renderer/src/components/LexiconCard.tsx)

**Follow-up verification: dictionary links and Word frequency.** The extended Hebrew entries now open directly from the interlinear and Words, and the vocabulary cards use their published headwords and glosses. In classic Strong’s, 312 references across 287 entries used leading zeros; all resolve to canonical dictionary IDs, including G5 (*Abba*) → H02 → H2 (Aramaic *father*). The separate Word frequency tool counts the components recorded in TAHOT and highlights the exact component within the source word. Its Masoretic count excludes reconstructions and uses the Qere reading once. The translation concordance and its historical tagging remain distinct. [Dictionary checks](../test/lexiconDirectory.test.ts) · [Frequency checks](../test/wordFrequency.test.ts)

**The 95% accuracy claim at audit time used some of its test data to construct its lookup tables.** The build collects which English forms and Strong’s numbers occur together from both tagged translations, including KJV. It evaluates on the same KJV verses and scores only words for which both the reference and the algorithm supply a tag. I repeated the evaluation with the actual derivation function and then excluded KJV token records from the lookup tables, retaining the BSB pivot and Strong’s dictionary usage definitions in both runs. [Lookup construction](../data-pipeline/build.ts#L446) · [Scoring rule](../data-pipeline/build.ts#L524)

| Lookup data | Correct / words with both tags | Agreement on comparable words | Correct / all 322,496 KJV words with a reference tag |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| BSB and KJV tokens | 229,818 / 241,631 | 95.11% | 71.26% |
| BSB tokens alone | 221,455 / 238,115 | 93.00% | 68.67% |

The paired runs used 789,642 KJV English word units. The reference leaves many words untagged. These measurements cannot establish accuracy or coverage for ASV, WEB, YLT, Julia Smith, Tyndale, Geneva or Wycliffe. The reader now identifies these word links as inferred. Future reporting should give the evaluation set, coverage and denominator. [Corpus attribution](attribution.md#derived-word-mappings)

**Quick Replace behavior at audit time.** With Quick Replace enabled, KJV 2 Chronicles 27:3 changed “He built the high gate” to “He built Elyon gate.” H5945 describes the height of a gate here. The term list treats it as a divine name and can also remove a neighboring article. A selection sent to the assistant used the rendered DOM string, allowing display substitutions to enter the saved transcript as quoted translation text. The database verse still contained the published wording. The historical catalog is available with `git show fc0a0fd:src/shared/quickReplace.ts`, line 70. The old selection code is also available via `git show fc0a0fd:src/renderer/src/components/ReadingPanel.tsx`.

The revised reader checks all 45 lexical entries from the historical catalog. Settings accept on/off choices. In BSB Psalm 23:1 the verified four-letter name displays as YHWH. In BSB John 3:16 “loved” displays as the recorded form *ēgapēsen*. Matthew 3:16 keeps “He” even though the publisher links the pronoun to the Greek name for Jesus. Ezekiel 37:1 keeps “by His Spirit” because the source form aligned to “Spirit” already includes the preposition. Copy and assistant selections retain the published English. [Source-word rendering](../src/shared/sourceWords.ts) · [Occurrence verification](../test/sourceWordIntegrity.test.ts)

With every term selected, the rebuilt BSB corpus shows YHWH at 2,941 occurrences and other forms at 3,432. The KJV shows YHWH at 1,916 occurrences. Its one additional verified display is *Iēsous* for “Jesus” in John 11:35. For these other terms, KJV requires identical verse wording, matching English span boundaries, an independent KJV tag and the same unique inflected form in the corresponding original-language witness. The other translations have no eligible restoration spans. The BSB links for Hebrew H1100 and H7854 supply no standalone, verified “Belial” or “Satan” eligible for display. ## Reproduce selected findings

From the repository root, open `sqlite3 -readonly resources/bible.sqlite`. These queries read the built corpus directly.

```sql
-- The two uses of “baptize” share a lexical number but disagree in person and voice.
SELECT edition, original, strongs, morph, gloss FROM original_tokens
WHERE edition IN ('NA','TR') AND book_id='1Cor'
  AND chapter=1 AND verse=15 AND strongs='G907';

-- The three texts identify the Proverbs reference by content.
SELECT translation_id, chapter, verse, text FROM verses
WHERE book_id='Prov' AND (
  (translation_id IN ('LXX','KJV') AND chapter=24 AND verse=27)
  OR (translation_id='KJV' AND chapter=30 AND verse=4)
);

-- An imported range whose second chapter was lost.
SELECT to_book, to_chapter, to_verse_start, to_verse_end FROM cross_references
WHERE from_book='Matt' AND from_chapter=5 AND from_verse=7
  AND to_book='Eph' AND to_chapter=4 AND to_verse_start=32;
```

For a source-side check after building the database, this command prints the four omitted Job subdivisions and their line counts of 11, 24, 9 and 36. The source files are build-cache inputs. The six cached STEPBible files matched the published Git blobs at commit `f71b5e51`. The cached Swete Job file matched `26bad3eb`. [STEPBible source snapshot](https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data/tree/f71b5e51f2d768ae72e3ed69743a7a9df25ef8b5/Translators%20Amalgamated%20OT%2BNT) · [Swete source snapshot](https://github.com/nathans/lxx-swete/tree/26bad3eb42bba98471d154c954e36a6f30a0279d/data)

## Verification limits and priorities

The independent word-order comparison used all 7,957 Scrivener and 7,953 Byzantine verse lines and normalized case, accents, punctuation and final sigma. It singled out whole verses with equal word multisets to isolate order differences. Other discrepancies can involve spelling, source conventions and versification and require separate adjudication. The NA/TR apparatus check compares the app’s two editions to each other. It does not independently certify the entire NA28 corpus. Assistant probes inspected citations and the context sent to a test provider through the packaged macOS application. The probes measured retrieval and source integrity. Model-generated prose quality remains unevaluated.

Prioritize source-preserving import and exact-reference safety, then validate each Greek edition and its apparatus against independent witnesses. Add regression cases for these passages alongside corpus-level comparisons. Existing tests exercised Psalm 51 Hebrew coverage, the end of Romans, Jude 1:5, Psalm mapping and basic assistant ranges. Each passed alongside the discrepancies catalogued here. The restoration tests now compare displayed words with the publisher’s bilingual rows across both BSB and KJV, exercise the term settings independently and check quoted selections. The other corpus, apparatus, mapping and exact-reference findings above remain open.
